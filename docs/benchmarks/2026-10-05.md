# 0.5.1rc1 local validation — October 5, 2026

This candidate builds on the installed but unpublished 0.5.0, retaining its mask-aware NVIDIA Optical Flow interpolation and current-frame XSeg. The first checkout located was 0.4.0; that experiment is not substituted for this final implementation. The existing 0.5.0 checkout and its pending Nix changes remain untouched. Numeric results are in [the accompanying JSON](2026-10-05.json).

## Supplied private clip

A consented 17.15-second talking/profile/microphone/hand clip was processed locally with the supplied face. No footage, source image, signing keys or identifying filenames are included in the repository or release.

0.5.0 selected 3× for a 29.999 FPS source, giving 89.998 FPS, 1,542 output frames and 44.76 s processing. Its 0.9 YuNet threshold missed the primary face in sampled brightly lit profile views. The candidate uses 0.6, largest-face selection in quality mode and a timing-tolerant 2× choice; explicit `--multi-face` remains available. Heuristic cache entries are recomputed to prevent reuse of the earlier 3× choice. Measured benchmark cache choices are preserved.

The final export has 514 keyframes, 513 generated frames and one final held frame: 1,028 at 59.998 FPS, 640×360. Median swap-stage time was 47.5 ms; FrameGen 11.4 ms/generated frame; total processing 36.8 s. This is offline processing, not a live FPS claim. Video duration is 17.133763 s; packet-bounded original AAC is 17.152 s (18.24 ms difference, less than one source frame). No measured perceptual lip-sync score is claimed.

Inspection shows face replacement in sampled talking/profile shots; conservative extreme-yaw handling leaves originals in others. Local target chroma reduces reliance on global LAB matching, but bright clipped lighting and partial-profile transitions can still show seams. No cinematic-grade identity or relighting claim is made.

A hand-interior polygon on frame 490 contained 2,921 pixels. The engine assigned exactly zero visible alpha to every annotated hand pixel while leaving 1,361 visible face pixels above alpha 0.1. This measures one selected interior region, not overall hand boundary IoU or every generated frame.

## Synthetic active-face performance

720p fictional portrait; 30 FPS pacing, eight-second runs; RTX 4090 / Torch 2.6.0+cu124; two-core Nix build and other GPU applications active.

| 2× FrameGen | 19.6 | 58.7 | 59.8 | 145.9 ms | 1,662 |
| 3× FrameGen | 19.5 | 89.7 | 90.1 | 153.5 ms | 1,652 |

2× had 15 held slots, 15 late drops and 83 dropped source frames. New output frames include flow-generated frames between processed keyframes and do not imply 30 unique source captures per second. Whole-device readings near 12.1 GiB include other processes; Torch allocation excludes ORT/driver allocations. Engine time was about 6.35 ms in the 2× snapshot, versus roughly 7–8 ms per known-mask fixture. These short-window, background-loaded figures are not an isolated maximum-capacity guarantee.

## Seven-object mask benchmark

126 moving frames on a fictional face with known stylized foreground shapes: hand, hair, glasses, microphone, cup, phone and full occlusion. Leakage was zero in all categories. Mask IoU ranged 0.4127–1.0; conservative false-positive occlusion reduced visible-face retention to 0.8352–0.9604 for partial fixtures. Boundary F1 was 0.3166–0.5322 for partial fixtures, showing that zero leakage is not perfect boundary quality. Static repeat alpha delta was zero. Moving alpha delta includes real object motion and is not labeled flicker.

## Encoded provenance

Actual final asset: valid C2PA signature and BMFF content binding, with an untrusted local development certificate, actions for face replacement/interpolation/encoding and IPTC `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`. TrustMark Q payload `DFv1AI` detected in 10/10 final encoded samples. It is an image watermark applied framewise, not SynthID or proof of authorship.

These results do not establish reliable H.264/social-media survival. Severe compression defeats this watermark, and ordinary remux/transcoding drops C2PA. No platform upload test was performed; X controls labeling. VideoSeal remains a researched video-specific alternative, not an installed or benchmarked backend.

## Checks and remaining coverage

Python: 70 passed. CUDA: both flow-direction and generated-foreground regressions passed. Nix x86_64 CPU package: 68 passed, two provenance dependency skips, two CUDA deselections; import checks passed. Ruff lint/format and wheel/sdist builds passed. The Nix pytest argument quoting and immutable OpenCV monkeypatch issues exposed by the real build were repaired. No global pip installation or automatic model download was used.

Final 120-source-frame webcam and virtualcam runs detected an active face track, performed AlphaFace compositing and started Quickshell/NVOF automatically. Webcam: input 24 FPS, swaps 24.0 FPS, new output 73.4 FPS, sink 73.9 FPS, measured pipeline latency 82.1 ms; 3× NVOF, six held and six late slots. Virtualcam last published IPC: input 24 FPS, swaps 23.9 FPS, output 71.6 FPS, latency 88.6 ms; 3× NVOF, widget running, `/dev/video10` writer completed. Short-window output can exceed nominal 72 FPS. Whole-device VRAM was about 11.9 GiB, including other processes. These measurements do not establish live hand/glasses/cup/phone correctness; no physical maneuver was witnessed. This candidate remains a prerelease until the broader requested real-object and lighting matrix is validated. Identity embedding accuracy, microexpression drift, true 3D pose, alpha-matte hair quality and camera-to-photon latency remain unmeasured.
